## LG's TV lineup right now, sorted by what you actually get

LG doesn't sell one "LG TV." It sells about six families, and the gap between the cheapest and the most expensive is roughly the price of a used car. Here's the honest split.

**OLED evo G6 (2026 flagship).** Screen sizes from 55 to 97 inches, with launch pricing starting at $2,499 in the US. This is the series with LG's Hyper Radiant Color Tech and Brightness Booster Ultra, which LG describes as up to 3.9x the brightness of a regular OLED, plus the Alpha 11 AI Processor Gen3. It also carries a 5-year panel warranty, which matters because OLED panels are the expensive part to replace. If you're wall-mounting, G-series is the one built for flush mounting.

**OLED evo C6 (2026 mainstream).** Six sizes from 42 to 83 inches, MSRP starting at $1,399. LG calls it its best-selling OLED, and for most people this is the correct answer. The C6 runs the same Alpha 11 Gen3 processor, supports Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos, and pushes 4K at up to 165Hz with VRR — up from 144Hz on the C5. The 77- and 83-inch C6H models use a tandem OLED panel.

**OLED B-series.** The B5 was 2025's entry OLED; the B6 is its replacement. A 65-inch B6 was listed at $1,199.99 during Labor Day sales this year against a $1,999.99 reference price. Fewer brightness tricks than the C-series, same fundamental OLED contrast, 120Hz panel, Dolby Vision, Dolby Atmos, and Nvidia G-Sync plus AMD FreeSync support. This is where the value conversation starts.

**Last year's OLEDs — G5, C5, B5.** Still on shelves, still worth a serious look, and often the best price-per-inch on the OLED floor. During LG's Memorial Day run this year, the 65-inch G5 sat at $2,199 (a stated $1,200 off MSRP), the 65-inch C5 at $1,299, and the 65-inch B5 at $999. Independent deal coverage later in the year found the 65-inch C5 at $1,299.99 and the 48-inch B5 down to $599.99 as retailers cleared stock.

**QNED / QNED evo (Mini LED LCD).** Sizes from 43 inches up to 100 inches, with the 2025 QNED range certified for 100% Color Volume. QNED evo adds Mini LED backlighting and higher refresh rates. You lose the per-pixel black of OLED and gain brightness headroom and a much lower price for large sizes. Small QNED sets start under $400; large Mini LED models run past $3,000.

**Gallery / LX7 Mini LED.** The framed, art-mode set with an attachable frame and LG Gallery+ access. Deal trackers had the 55-inch LX7B at $699.99 and the 65-inch at $899.99 down from $1,299.99 and $1,799.99 respectively.

## Where LG TV prices actually come from

Three levers, in order of how much money they move.

**Model year turnover.** LG announced its 2026 G6 and C6 pre-orders in March, with retail availability starting the same month. Every time that happens, the previous generation becomes the discount. A 65-inch C5 at $1,299.99 against a 65-inch C6 at $2,699 MSRP is the whole story in one line.

**Retailer clearance and coupons.** Best Buy, Amazon, eBay storefronts, and regional chains all compete for the same old stock. A Labor Day coupon this year put a 65-inch C6 at $1,199.99 through an authorized reseller on eBay — $300 under what everyone else was charging. That kind of thing happens for days, not weeks.

**Manufacturer promos.** LG.com periodically bundles free installation, a free wall mount, or a credit with an eligible TV purchase. These stack with sale pricing because they're structured as add-ons, not discounts on the set.

Cashback sits after all three, which is exactly why it's worth setting up before you buy rather than after.
